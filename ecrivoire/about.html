Why a purple cow

Safe is invisible.
Remarkable gets talked about.

Drive past a field of brown cows and you don't look twice. Drive past one purple cow and you pull over, take a photo, and tell everyone you know. That's the whole idea behind this shop — "purple cow" marketing thinking, borrowed shamelessly and applied literally: in a city where every good bookstore has learned the same quiet, minimal playbook, being genuinely remarkable is worth more than being merely competent.

So instead of another beige interior, we built thick black borders, hard drop shadows, and a mascot family that lives in the margins of every page. Inkling isn't a logo — she's a promise that this shop is trying, on purpose, to be impossible to confuse with anywhere else.

And no, the shop isn't actually painted purple. Inkling doesn't need to be either — the whole point of "purple cow" thinking is that remarkable isn't a colour, it's a decision. Ours is black, white and every grey in between, on purpose, the same way everything else here is: loudly, and by choice.

Our story

From a spare
room to a press

Odile Ferrand started Écrivoire Studios in 2025 with a sewing frame, a board shear bought secondhand, and a simple question: why does every "classic" reprint look the same? We began online, selling numbered editions of public-domain texts we'd designed and bound ourselves — no shopfront, just a studio and a monthly print run.

A physical shopfront is the plan, once the catalogue and the waitlist both earn it. Until then, every edition ships from the studio, and every original manuscript we publish goes through the same hands that publish the classics.

How we got here

A short timeline

Early 2025

Odile sets up a sewing frame in a spare room and binds a test run of ten books.

August 2025

Édition No. 01 — Pride and Prejudice — goes up online and sells out in three weeks.

Late 2025

Théo and Delphine join full-time; the monthly-drop schedule becomes official.

Early 2026

Publish With Us opens; the first original manuscript is accepted within a month.

Today

Nineteen editions printed, four people on staff, one physical shopfront still to come.
