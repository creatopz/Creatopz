Why a purple cow

Safe is invisible.
Remarkable gets talked about.

Drive past a field of brown cows and you don't look twice. Drive past one purple cow and you pull over, take a photo, and tell everyone you know. That's the whole idea behind this shop — "purple cow" marketing thinking, borrowed shamelessly and applied literally: in a city where every good bookstore has learned the same quiet, minimal playbook, being genuinely remarkable is worth more than being merely competent.

So instead of another beige interior, we built thick black borders, hard drop shadows, and a mascot family that lives in the margins of every page. Inkling isn't a logo — she's a promise that this shop is trying, on purpose, to be impossible to confuse with anywhere else.

And no, the shop isn't actually painted purple. Inkling doesn't need to be either — the whole point of "purple cow" thinking is that remarkable isn't a colour, it's a decision. Ours is black, white and every grey in between, on purpose, the same way everything else here is: loudly, and by choice.

Our story

From print shop
to bookshop

Odile Ferrand opened Écrivoire Studios in 2014, in the ground floor of a building that had spent sixty years as a commercial print shop. The old board shear is still in the back room. So is the smell of the place — paper, glue, and the particular dust of a building that has always made things by hand.

What started as a small fiction and poetry shelf grew, slowly and on purpose, into eight curated categories, a working bindery, and a reading series that now fills the main room twice a month. We have never opened a second location, and don't plan to — we would rather know every book on the shelf.

How we got here

A short timeline

2014

Écrivoire opens with one room, two shelves, and a hand-painted sign.

2017

The bindery opens in the back room; the first Saturday workshop runs with six students.

2020

The reading series begins, one Friday a month, in what used to be the storeroom.

2024

The shelves are reorganised by feeling instead of genre. It sticks.

Today

Four booksellers, eight categories, one very full events calendar.
